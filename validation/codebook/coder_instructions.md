# Verifying the VLM classification of maps — briefing

English translation of the Polish original (2 September 2026), the briefing the two
coders read before they started. In case of doubt the Polish original prevails. The email
address is left as it stood in the original.

## 1. Purpose

For every map you answer **twelve questions about what that map is**:

- **three about the nature of the map** — whether it is a statistical map, whether the
  data are referred to administrative or statistical units, whether the data are numeric;

- **seven about the presentation method** — whether the map is a choropleth, a diagram
  map, an isarithmic map, a dot density map, a heat map, a cartogram (value-by-area or
  geometric), a flow map. One map may carry more than one presentation method;

- **two about the content of the sheet** — whether there is any text on the map, and what
  the treatment of the data is (stepped = divided into classes; continuous = not divided
  into classes).

The maps come from the StatMapCorpus corpus, in which these same properties were
described automatically beforehand by a VLM. Your answers will serve as **validation
material** for the descriptions produced by the AI.

## 2. Scope

You have **157 maps** to assess. They appear one at a time, in random order.
Progress is saved after every committed map, so the work can be interrupted at any point
and resumed on another device after logging in — you return to the map where you left
off.

## 3. Basis of the judgement

You decide **solely on the basis of the image shown**. You do not look the map up on the
internet or in any other source.

The quality of the cartographic work and the aesthetics of the map are **not** under
assessment. You are identifying **what the map is**, not how well it was made.

## 4. Answer scale

**Every field requires a choice** — there is no default value, and a map cannot be
committed with a field left unresolved.

In most fields you have three options available: **yes**, **no** or **cannot assess**.

"Cannot assess" is reserved for the situation in which the image makes the judgement
impossible: the element is illegible at this resolution, cut off by the frame, or written
in a script you do not read.

The question about the **treatment of the data** has a different set of three options.
You decide it from the legend — that is what shows whether the values were divided into
classes:

1) **stepped** — the legend shows distinct classes: separate patches with ranges, or a
  bar with a visible boundary between colour steps;

2) **continuous** — a smooth bar with no boundaries, labelled only at the ends;

3) **qualitative / no legend / cannot assess** — one answer for every situation in which
  that distinction does not apply: qualitative data, no legend, or an illegible legend.

When you hesitate between stepped and continuous and you can see even one distinct
boundary between colour steps — that is **stepped**.

## 5. Presentation methods

The seven methods are **seven independent judgements**, not one choice from a list. Maps
may combine more than one presentation method — a choropleth plus a diagram map, for
instance. For each method you mark separately whether it occurs on the map at hand.

A map may fit none of the seven methods. In that case you answer "no" to each of them.

## 6. Useful information

These instructions, together with the detailed descriptions of the individual questions
and options, are available alongside every map by clicking the "Instructions" button in
the top left corner of the screen.

Once you have clicked "Commit map" you cannot go back to that map, so make sure every
field is marked correctly.

Clicking on the map image lets you enlarge or rotate it, should you need to.

If you are unsure how to resolve a given field, read the instructions first (the
"Instructions" button above the map), and if that does not help, contact me
(m.solarz2@uw.edu.pl).
